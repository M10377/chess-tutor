# 3. Deep dives: the highest-impact capabilities

Each section: the benchmark experience step by step, where we fall short (with the test that shows it), and what our version should do, in enough detail to become a spec.

## 3.1 Mistake review that teaches (review + retry + hints + explanation)

**Benchmark: chess.com Game Review** (documented in chess.com support "Game Review", checked 2026-11-14; free tier 1 full review/day, Coach on all moves = Diamond). Not tested hands-on (needs login; manual check 1 in `tests.md`).

1. Game ends, "Game Review" button on the result pop-up.
2. Highlights: eval graph, accuracy, one-line Coach summary.
3. Step through moves; each has a class (Brilliant, Great, Best, Excellent, Good, Book, Inaccuracy, Mistake, Miss, Blunder) and Coach text that updates per move.
4. "Next" jumps between key moments. The first is usually the last book move, with the opening name, how often you play it, and a study suggestion.
5. On a key mistake the bubble offers graduated reveals ("Show Fork", "Show Checkmate", "Show Idea") before "Best".
6. "Retry" resets the position; a correct move shows its score, a wrong one gets a Coach explanation.

**Our app today** (`tests.md` G1/G2): the summary, labels, graph and green best-move arrow are at parity and unlimited. Gaps:

- No key-moment navigation; the player scans a 40-row move list.
- No retry: the board is view-only (`Replay.tsx`). The same position comes back days later as a puzzle, which is good for retention but misses the moment of highest motivation.
- No in-app "why". 5...Bxd1 shows "Mistake · best was dxe5 · Eval M2 · engine line Bxf7+ Ke7 Nd5#". A 950 player has to infer "I allowed mate" from "M2".
- Label error: allowing mate in 2 is a "Mistake".

**Proposed experience** (spec for `first-slice.md`):

1. After analysis, show "**Your 3 key moments**": the player's mistakes/blunders/misses ordered by win-% lost, capped at 3 for games under 40 moves and 5 above. Each card: move number, what you played, severity.
2. Tapping a card puts the board **before** the mistake, the player's colour at the bottom, prompt "You played Bxd1 here. Find something better." Board is interactive.
3. Player moves:
   - If the move is the engine's best or within the puzzle tolerance (4 win-%), "Yes! dxe5 keeps you in the game." plus the one-line explanation.
   - Otherwise a **one-line refutation** built from the engine: "After Bxd1, White has Bxf7+ Ke7 Nd5#: a forced mate." Then "Try again" or a hint.
4. **Hint ladder**, three levels, never auto-advancing: (1) what to look at, no piece or square ("There is a forced checkmate in 2 against you. Look at f7 and your king's escape squares" is *not* allowed: it names a square; use "Look at what your opponent threatens against your king."); (2) the piece ("Use a pawn."); (3) the move with arrow and main line. Each step used is recorded.
5. **Explanation** at the depth the player chose in settings (Short = 1 sentence; Normal = up to 3 sentences with the key line; Deep = plus alternatives and the refutation line). Built by the rule-based tutor (no model). Example generated by the prototype for G1: "Bxd1 is a serious mistake: it allows a forced checkmate, Bxd1 Bxf7+ Ke7 Nd5#. Better is dxe5 (-2.16 pawns for you), main line dxe5 Qxg4 Nc6 d3. Next: Do 10 mate-in-2 puzzles."
6. Buttons under the explanation: "Ask Claude" / "Ask ChatGPT" (board-aware handoff, 3.3 path 3), and, only if a local model is configured, "Explain more" (3.3 path 2).
7. On leaving the card, the position is (already) in the puzzle queue; its SRS state starts from whether the retry succeeded without hints.
8. Labels: a move that turns a non-losing position into a forced mate for the opponent is a **Blunder** whatever the win-% drop; add **Miss** when the player had a forced mate or a >= 30 win-% gain available and played a move that keeps less than half of it.

## 3.2 Tactics practice at the right level

**Benchmark: lichess puzzles** (hands-on 2026-11-14 anonymous: puzzle 0LEQK loads, rating hidden until solved, multi-move; documented: Glicko-2 per player and per puzzle, 100+ themes, dashboard by theme, CC0 database of 6,193,530 puzzles with rating, RD, popularity, themes and opening tags).

1. Open Puzzles; a puzzle near your rating loads, opponent's last move is animated.
2. Solve a multi-move line; wrong move = fail but you can continue; "good but not best" alternatives accepted.
3. Rating updates; next puzzle; theme pages let you drill one motif; dashboard shows accuracy by theme.

**chess.com free**: 3 rated puzzles/day (support article). **Chess Tempo free**: unlimited rated tactics, theme stats; SRS only on Gold.

**Our app**: only own-mistake puzzles (fixtures yield 1 per 2 games), single-move, no rating, no theme, no hint.

**Proposed**:

1. Ship a static, pre-filtered slice of the lichess CC0 file: puzzles with Popularity >= 85, NbPlays >= 500, RD <= 90, rating 400 to 1800 (Micah's band plus headroom), about 50,000 rows split into rating-band JSON chunks of about 100 KB gzipped each, loaded on demand. Attribution: "Puzzles from the lichess.org database (CC0)".
2. A per-player rating (Glicko-2, start at chess.com puzzle rating if the player enters it, else 800), and puzzle choice within +-150 of it, avoiding repeats.
3. Multi-move validation shared with own-game puzzles: accept the stored line, or any move whose engine eval is within tolerance (existing logic).
4. Same 3-level hint ladder. Using a hint caps the result at "solved with help" (no rating gain).
5. Theme filter: the 12 themes most relevant below 1400 (hangingPiece, fork, pin, skewer, mateIn1, mateIn2, backRankMate, discoveredAttack, doubleCheck, trappedPiece, promotion, defensiveMove).
6. Daily queue mixes: due own-game puzzles first, then 10 rated puzzles.

## 3.3 The three tutor paths (Micah's spec, 2026-11-14)

Spec: first usable version costs $0 beyond what Micah already pays; works in the app without a paid API or hosted backend; player controls explanation depth and hint spoilers; useful without any model; chat handoff always available; paid providers optional and not in the first release.

All three paths are scored on the same 32 tutor tasks (`fixtures/tutor-tasks.json`, built from the 22 engine/tablebase-checked fixtures) with the same automatic checks (`tools/tutor-score.mjs`): unsupported moves named, hint spoiler level, illegal-move/false-premise handling (T4), overclaiming a draw as a win (T6). Teaching quality is my manual 0-3 rating with the anchors in `tests.md`; that is one rater's judgement and is labelled as such. Results: § 3.6 and `results/`.

### Path 1: built-in explanations and hints (no model)

What it is: `tools/rule-tutor.mjs` (prototype). From Stockfish lines + chess.js it detects: check, capture, mate and mate-in-N, back-rank and smothered mate, fork/double attack (piece attacks two targets that are higher value or undefended), pin and skewer (sliding-piece rays), sacrifice (moved piece is captured next), insufficient material at the end of the line, opponent's double attack after a proposed move, illegal proposals and material count. It fills short templates at three depths and a three-level hint ladder, plus a per-motif "practise next" line. Endgames use a small authored plan library (4 entries today) plus the tablebase verdict.

### Path 2: optional local model on the Mac (Ollama)

What it is: the same grounded prompt (`tools/tutor-prompt.mjs`) sent to a small model running on the Mac through Ollama on `localhost:11434`. The app would call it from the browser; that requires starting Ollama with `OLLAMA_ORIGINS=https://m10377.github.io` (CORS) and only works on the Mac where it runs. Results from Micah's M4 / 24 GB: § 3.6.

### Path 3: board-aware chat handoff

What it is: the existing "Ask Claude" button, upgraded. One click copies and opens a prompt with the current FEN, side to move, the move being asked about, the engine's top lines with evals (MultiPV 3), the refutation of the player's move, the level, the chosen depth, and a hint-only instruction when the player asked for a hint. Claude: `https://claude.ai/new?q=` (already used by the app). ChatGPT: `https://chatgpt.com/?q=` prefill (widely used, **not verified in this benchmark**; fallback is clipboard + open chatgpt.com). Follow-ups happen in the chat with the player's own subscription. Cost $0 extra.

### What each path is good at

| Criterion | Path 1 rules | Path 2 local model | Path 3 chat handoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Correctness (named moves supported by engine/legal) | Guaranteed by construction | Measured, see 3.6 | Not checkable by the app (answer stays in the chat) |
| Teaching quality | Correct but formulaic; weak level adaptation | Measured, see 3.6 | Strongest prose and follow-ups (Claude/ChatGPT), unverified on fixtures |
| Latency | Under 10 ms per answer (measured) | Measured, see 3.6 | Seconds, plus switching apps |
| Setup | None | Install Ollama, pull a model (2.5 to 5 GB), set CORS env var | None (needs a Claude or ChatGPT account, which Micah has) |
| Mobile | Works everywhere | Mac only; not on phone | Works on phone (opens the app/site) |
| Cost | $0 | $0 (electricity) | $0 beyond existing plan |
| Hint spoiler control | Exact (levels are generated separately) | Depends on model obedience | Depends on model obedience |

## 3.4 Explanation depth and spoiler controls (applies to all paths)

Settings, stored locally:

- **Explanation depth**: Short / Normal / Deep (default Normal). Path 1 template length, path 2/3 word limits from `tutor-prompt.mjs`.
- **Hints**: "Hints first" (default: player must ask; level 1 shown first), "Show answer right away" (for quick review), "No hints" (pure test).
- **Spoiler guard**: in hint mode, path 1 is structurally safe; for paths 2 and 3 the app also runs `score()`'s spoiler check on local-model output and replaces a spoiling hint with the path 1 hint (path 3 cannot be checked, so the handoff prompt says "Give ONE hint only..." and the button is labelled "Ask Claude for a hint").

## 3.5 Beginner fundamentals tied to the player's mistakes

**Benchmarks**: lichess /learn and /practice (hands-on, free, anonymous): short interactive lessons and play-to-goal exercises in 18 practice topics including checkmate patterns, pin, fork, skewer, discovered attack, pawn endgames. Duolingo Chess (vendor blog): free, about 75% puzzles plus games against "Oscar", up to about 1500, with spaced repetition.

**Our gap**: none of this exists; but copying lichess is pointless when it is free.

**Proposed**: no general curriculum. Instead, when the motif tagger (path 1 detectors run over the player's own mistakes) sees the same motif 3 times in 10 games, unlock a 5-minute micro-lesson for it: one worked example (engine-verified), two near-transfer positions from the CC0 set with that theme, one far-transfer position where the pattern does not work (like L1's third step), then the player's own original mistake as the final exercise. Link to the matching lichess /practice topic for more. Content per lesson: about 1 hour to author and verify with `verify-fixtures.mjs`.
